About Us

Led by the diaspora. Built for Nigeria.

We are a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization led by the diaspora, built for Nigeria, and rooted in the belief that when hearts agree — lasting change follows.

Our Origin

A promise made in Lagos.

In 2023, our founder stood in a shelter in Lagos, Nigeria, surrounded by women who had survived things most people will never have to imagine. She had come to give. What she left with was a promise.

She promised to come back. To bring more. To make sure these women — and others like them across Nigeria — would know that someone on the other side of the world had not forgotten them.

Read the full origin story →

Hearts Agree Foundation is the fulfillment of that promise. It is what happens when memory becomes action.

Our Name

What "Hearts Agree" means.

Our name comes from the direct translation of Obiekwe — the founding family name — meaning "hearts agree." It is a name rooted in consensus, in collective will, and in the Igbo understanding that the most important decisions are made not by one, but by many.

We believe the diaspora and Nigeria are not in tension. They are in agreement. What we build together is what neither could build alone.

Mission

Hearts Agree Foundation builds the ecosystem — the skills, resources, and coordinated resolve — that turns one-off diaspora generosity into the people, programs, and infrastructure that create lasting change in Nigeria.

Vision

A Nigeria where the distance between diaspora and home is not measured in miles, but in the impact made across them. Where investment, memory, and love are indistinguishable from one another.

Philosophy

There is a difference between charity and investment. Charity asks nothing of the future. Investment builds it. We show up because we remember — and because memory, for us, is productive. It moves us to act.

Our Founder

Maya Nneka Obiekwe

Maya Nneka Obiekwe is a community builder, advocate, and strategist with over fifteen years of experience creating infrastructure for women — professionally, philanthropically, and personally.

She is the founder of Black Women Rising, a professional community of over 500 members that has been featured in the New York Times, Good Morning America, Rolling Stone, and CNBC. She has spent her career at the intersection of advocacy, community organizing, and systems-building — from founding one of the first student-led sexual assault response programs on a college campus, to leading go-to-market strategy for healthcare data and AI products at a national consulting firm.

She goes by Ada Orimili — daughter of the great water. Hearts Agree Foundation is her father's question made real.
